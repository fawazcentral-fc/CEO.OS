# CEO OS: First-Version Brief

Working name: CEO OS
For: Fawaz first. Later, founders whose brains never stop.
Status: Hypothesis. Revise after 30 days of real use.

## The problem

My thinking has nowhere to live except my head. New ideas compete with committed
work. Past thinking and decisions scatter across apps and get lost, so I reason
from scratch and cannot tell whether an old decision should still stand.

The return fails from scatter, not from lack of habit. When I do find old
thinking, it is valuable. So the core job is: one place, always findable.

## Philosophy

One Constraint. One Number. One Move.
Capturing an idea is never committing to it.
The core must be useful without AI. AI, if ever, is optional and invoked on purpose.

## Core user moment

Any hour, often 2am. Something is on my mind: a vent, a worry, an idea, a
problem, a decision I am avoiding. I open one place and write. No fields, no
category, no business tab. Weeks later I wonder "have I thought about this
before?" and I open the same place, search, and find it.

Second moment it serves: a new idea lands while I am on committed work. I see
the constraint I am serving, note that the idea does not move it, park it in one
line, and carry on.

## Daily loop

1. Write anything in the journal. Zero structure at write time.
2. Optionally lift a line out into a thread, a decision, or an idea. The
   original stays untouched.
3. Glance at the current constraint and one move for the business that has me
   this week.

Weekly, in under 15 minutes: is the constraint still the constraint? What
evidence? Did the one move happen? Any decision whose change condition has been
met?

## Essential screens (four, no more)

1. Journal. One stream, newest first, timestamped, full-text search. Optional
   tags after the fact: business, idea, decision, problem. Nothing has a
   checkbox.
2. Constraint. Per business: the binding constraint in a sentence, the evidence
   that makes it binding, the one number, the one move. Plus a short queue of
   known next constraints. Parked ideas can attach to a constraint, current or
   queued.
3. Decisions. What I decided, why, what would justify changing it, date.
   Optionally linked to the journal entry it came from.
4. Overview. One answer: which business gets my best hours this week, and why.
   The others are in maintenance mode by choice. Not a dashboard.

## Structure

Self is the journal. It is the foundation and the raw material.
Businesses are tags and a constraint record, not tabs with five sections.
CEO Capacity and CEO Leadership are later layers built on the journal.

## Deferred, on purpose

- Numbers beyond the one number per business.
- Any AI.
- Per-business thought sections, dashboards, charts.
- Multi-founder accounts, sharing, product infrastructure.
- Tasks, reminders, notifications. A vent never becomes a task.
- Mobile app. Must work well in a phone browser at 2am, that is enough.

## How we know it is helping: the one number

Candidates considered:

- Entries written. Rewards volume. More notes is not the goal. Rejected.
- Time in app. Rewards fiddling. Rejected.
- Decisions recorded with reasons. Measures input. Some weeks have none. Secondary.
- Derailments avoided. Self-reported and shame-prone. Unreliable. Rejected.
- Times I searched for old thinking and found it. Exact pain, but self-reported. Secondary.
- Returns: times I reopened or added to an entry older than 7 days. Automatic,
  no self-report, baseline is near zero today. Measures the behaviour that is
  broken. Weakness: it measures return, not follow-through.

Chosen: Returns to entries older than 7 days, counted per week.
No target. Measure the baseline for 30 days first. Follow-through becomes the
measure only after return is proven.

## Open hypotheses to settle in the first weeks of use

- Does parking an idea give relief on its own, or does my brain need a stated
  return point before it lets go?
- Can I name a binding constraint for each business in one sentence? If not,
  the constraint screen is the first thing I use, not the journal.
- Does the weekly review happen without a reminder? If not, decide whether a
  single weekly prompt is acceptable or a slide toward a task manager.
